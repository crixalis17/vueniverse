WhyPulse Winning Audit

Is the concept novel, useful, and feasible enough to win OpenAI Build Week with two builders and one week?

Verdict

Winning-capable, but not winning-safe yet. The idea is novel and user-appealing enough to contend. The win depends on making one event-to-evidence-to-action loop unforgettable—not implementing more connectors, previews, or model machinery.

Strong human hook

“My body reacts before this specific meeting” is immediate, personal, and easy to understand.

Credible novelty

Minute-level event alignment, matched personal controls, inspectable evidence, and local model interpretation form a defensible combination.

Main gap

The app currently explains a pattern well, but the user’s “what do I do next?” moment is mostly a Preview.

Main risk

Four native integrations, two platforms, local inference, an Explorer, many previews, and background analysis are too much for one week.

The one missing feature worth adding

Track the Unknown. Do not build a general experiment engine. Let the user resolve one missing factor and watch the evidence change.

What to cut from the critical path

Explorer before Explainer

Cut from SHIP. The Explorer is harder to prove and less visible than a reliable evidence explanation. Add it only after the Explainer works.

True background scheduling

Cut from SHIP. Run analysis on app open and manual refresh. Background execution is invisible in a three-minute demo.

Every preview as a full screen

Cut from SHIP. Use one feature-catalogue template; deeply mock only Ask, Track This, and Replay.

Two live detectors

Cut from SHIP. Make meeting → HR excellent. Keep late activity → sleep as a seeded second example.

Final recommendation

Build fewer things, but make the evidence move. WhyPulse is appealing and novel enough to win if judges can see one repeated body response, inspect why it exists, challenge a missing explanation, and watch the conclusion update. Do not add another connector or large feature after this.
